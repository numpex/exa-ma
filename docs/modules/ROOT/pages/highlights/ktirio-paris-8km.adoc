[.lead]
Cemosis ran Ktirio Urban Building on *32,768 MPI ranks* on the EuroHPC Discoverer supercomputer for the Paris 8 km case.

*Benchmark team.* Cemosis at IRMA (Université de Strasbourg and CNRS).

The run simulated *207,624 buildings* on a detailed mesh of *68,207,038 triangles*, split into *32,768 partitions*.
It traced *471,821,112 solar rays* and took *14 min 55 s* of wall time on Discoverer, with field output disabled.

== The Paris 8 km domain

The case covers an *8 km radius* around Notre-Dame.
The videos and images on this page are ParaView visualisations of the computational mesh.
In the flyover and the city views below, the mesh is coloured by land use. The legend identifies categories such as water, roads and parks.

== Why solar masks matter at city scale

A solar mask uses ray intersections to estimate how much of a surface can receive direct sunlight.
Evaluating more points on each face increases the number of queries, and an occluding building can belong to a different part of the distributed mesh.
This is one reason mesh partitioning and communication matter for the Ktirio workflow.

Both views show the area around Notre-Dame on *15 July 2025 at 09:00 in Paris (UTC+2)*.
The sun is almost due east, *86.7° clockwise from north* and *26.5° above the horizon*.
The camera looks towards Notre-Dame from the southeast. The *N* arrow points north and the *☀* arrow points towards the sun in the plane of the map.

The reference ignores obstructions, so yellow shows surfaces whose angle allows them to receive direct sun.
In the view with cast shadows, yellow indicates that all seven sample rays reach the sun, while dark purple indicates that buildings block all seven.
Blue-grey marks surfaces whose angle prevents them from receiving direct sun at this time. It has the same meaning in both views.
The calculation uses ray tracing on the Paris 8 km LoD1 mesh. Only building surfaces can block rays in this example.

== Distributed ray intersections

https://github.com/feelpp/feelpp/releases/tag/v0.112.0[Feel{plus}{plus} 0.112.0] adds top-level (TLAS) and bottom-level (BLAS) acceleration structures for fast ray intersections across many MPI ranks.
The top level identifies the mesh partitions a ray may hit, and the bottom level tests it against triangles on those ranks.

== Collaboration and sources

Ktirio Urban Building is developed by Cemosis at the University of Strasbourg as the Urban Building pilot of the European HiDALGO2 Centre of Excellence.
It is also a demonstrator for NumPEx.
