[.lead]
Clément Guillet and colleagues developed a solver that speeds up detailed simulations of how lattice materials deform under load.
In a 3D compression test with 864 cells, it ran about *five times faster* and used roughly *half the memory* of the standard solver.

== How the solver uses repeating cells

Lattice materials are built from many similar cells. Under large deformations, those cells can respond differently depending on their location in the structure.
The solver divides the lattice into subdomains and selects a small set of representative cells at each Newton iteration.
It uses those cells to approximate the local tangent operators throughout the lattice, retaining the fine-scale geometry in the simulation.

== A 3D compression test

The authors compressed a lattice with 864 cells and 526,737 degrees of freedom by 10%.
The reported execution times sum assembly and solve for the standard solver and initialization and solve for the proposed solver. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.10741[Open manuscript (Table 3)]
